ERDŐS–SELFRIDGE · SEPTEMBER 14, 2026

Further values of the Erdős–Selfridge function

The function g(k) is the least integer n > k+1 for which the binomial coefficient C(n,k) has no prime factor at most k. This release gives the complete table through k = 400 and records 23 computed values beyond the previously published frontier of 377.

Method

Following Sorenson, Sorenson and Webster, we choose a wheel from partial prime powers. For each prime p ≤ k, Kummer’s theorem requires every base-p digit of k to be at most the corresponding digit of n. CRT combines the selected constraints; residual filters check all remaining constraints. Each CUDA thread generates a strip of residues, amortizing the cost of CRT reconstruction.

For wheel modulus N, complete blocks [tN,(t+1)N) are scanned in increasing t. All surviving candidates are checked with the frozen integer reference. The smallest valid candidate in the first nonempty block is the result. A second run uses a different wheel and modulus. The last confirmations for 397 and 399 used word-mask filters with original candidate identities and compatible checkpoint coverage retained.

What was checked

On September 14, 2026, the release validator freshly checked all 400 values using Kummer’s digit condition and exact binomial coefficients, performing 17,391 prime-divisor tests. It also checked 108 unique completed block records from 46 runs for 378–400: consecutive coverage, wheel-derived moduli and residue counts, survivor intervals, hashes, and the absence of earlier valid survivors in the recorded lists.

These checks have different scopes. Exact witness checks establish admissibility. Minimality relies on the completeness and correctness of the recorded scans. The two wheels share software components, and survivor hashes do not certify that a kernel omitted nothing. This release audit did not rerun the large exhaustive searches independently.

Reproducibility and attribution

Evidence, exact checks, and replay instructions accompany the complete table. GPU timings are not compared across terms because the hardware, kernels and resumed work differ. No inference of a distributional law is made from this update.
